# SubmersedVR 1.0.0

A continuation of **SubmersedVR** for **Subnautica**, created to provide a deeper level of immersion in the game's underwater world.

This project is based on the work of **[Okabintaro](https://github.com/Okabintaro)**, the author of the original **[SubmersedVR](https://github.com/Okabintaro/SubmersedVR)**.

> **Huge thanks to Okabintaro.**  
> SubmersedVR is the foundation of this project. Motion controller support, VR interaction, hand and tool controls, PDA adaptations, and the overall architecture that made Subnautica properly playable in VR all exist thanks to his work and the contributions of the original project's contributors.

---

## What this mod does

SubmersedVR adds full motion controller support and a wide range of VR mechanics to the original **Subnautica**.

# Features

## Physical Swimming

One of the biggest additions is a fully physical swimming model.

When Phisical Swimming mode is enabled:

- **Swimming with your hands**
  - Player movement is generated by actual controller/hand movements.
  - Strokes are evaluated based on their speed and direction.
  - Both hands contribute to movement independently.

- **Natural inertia**
  - Each stroke accelerates the player, which means maintaining speed is easier than building it up from a complete stop.
  - After a stroke, the player continues to glide through the water.
  - Momentum gradually decreases depending on the configured water resistance.
  - Sideways strokes can rotate the player.

- **Fin Kick**
  - Fin Kick simulates propulsion from the player's legs. By holding both grip buttons on the controllers, the player can slowly move in the direction they are looking. Fin Kick strength can be configured separately.
  - Fin Kick speed is added to the speed generated by hand swimming. You can swim with your hands and legs at the same time.

- **Gestures**
  - Emergency ascent - lower your hands and extend them down alongside your body.
  - Emergency descent - raise your hands above you with the palms facing each other.
  - Underwater braking - hold your hands in front of you as if your palms were pushing against an invisible wall.

## Reworked Seaglide

The Seaglide now behaves more like a real device physically held by the player. This allows you to point the Seaglide in one direction while looking somewhere else.

- Movement direction is determined by the **orientation of the Seaglide itself**, independently of where the player is looking.
- The Seaglide motor receives an analog input from the controllers, allowing you to increase or decrease its speed. Fully press both grip buttons on the
  controllers to reach 100% thrust.
- Existing momentum is preserved and gradually reduced using the same water resistance model as normal swimming.

---

## PDA Radio / local music player

- Dedicated **Radio** tab inside the PDA.
- Icon and appearance styled to match the native PDA interface.
- Browse your local music library directly in the game.
- MP3 and FLAC are supported. These are the formats I have personally tested, but other formats may work as well.
- The music library can be stored in the mod's default `Music` folder or in an external directory.
- The path to an external library is configured in:
  - `BepInEx/config/SubmersedVR/music-library-path.txt`
- While Radio is playing, Subnautica's background music is disabled. When Radio playback stops, the game music is restored.

Default location for music used by the Subnautica Music Player:

```text
Subnautica/
└── BepInEx/
    └── plugins/
        └── SubmersedVR/
            └── Music/
```

Music can be organized into subfolders. When the mod starts, it scans the library and automatically builds an index of stations and tracks.

## Settings

### Physical Swimming

Player-facing settings are focused on a small set of understandable parameters. Below is a more detailed explanation of each one:

- **Stroke Strength**
  Controls the overall strength of hand strokes. The higher the value, the more strongly each full stroke accelerates the player.
  If swimming feels too slow and you have to move your arms too often, increase this value. If even a small stroke accelerates the player too aggressively, decrease it.

- **Water Resistance**
  Controls how quickly your speed decreases after a stroke. Essentially, this is the amount of water resistance affecting inertia and glide duration.
  At lower values, the player keeps their speed longer and glides farther. At higher values, the player slows down faster after each stroke.
  This setting affects movement decay, not the strength of the stroke itself.

- **Turn Strength**
  Controls how strongly sideways hand movements rotate the player.
  At low values, a sideways stroke mostly moves the player laterally. The higher the value, the more the same gesture rotates the player's body.
  At a value of 0, physical turning is completely disabled and sideways strokes work only as strafing movement.

- **Fin Kick Strength**
  Controls the strength of the additional acceleration produced by Fin Kick.
  Fin Kick is used as an additional movement method and is combined with normal hand swimming rather than replacing it.
  Higher values provide a more noticeable impulse and allow the player to build up speed faster, especially when travelling across open water.

## Diagnostics and telemetry

This continuation includes diagnostic tools that were used to tune physical swimming and investigate movement problems.

If diagnostic recording is enabled, the data is saved to:

```text
Subnautica/BepInEx/logs/SubmersedVR/
```

Typical files:

```text
physical-swimming-*.csv
physical-swimming-diagnostic-*.csv
```

These files are useful when submitting bug reports for complex movement problems such as unexpected acceleration, failed braking, or incorrect locomotion state transitions.

The standard BepInEx log is located here:

```text
Subnautica/BepInEx/LogOutput.log
```

# Installation

## Important: you do NOT need to install the original SubmersedVR separately

This repository already contains the continued version of SubmersedVR.

Installing both the original SubmersedVR and this continuation at the same time will result in duplicate and conflicting plugins.

You only need:

1. Subnautica.
2. BepInEx for Subnautica.
3. The release package **from this repository**.
4. SteamVR/OpenVR.

---

## Requirements

### Supported game version

The original SubmersedVR is designed for the PC version of Subnautica from Steam/Epic and uses VR through OpenVR/SteamVR.

### BepInEx

Install **BepInEx for Subnautica**:

https://github.com/toebeann/BepInEx.Subnautica

The original SubmersedVR documentation uses the Subnautica-specific BepInEx package rather than the standard generic BepInEx archive.

### SteamVR / OpenVR

The mod uses SteamVR/OpenVR.

The Oculus Runtime by itself is not supported by SubmersedVR's motion controls implementation.

For Quest / Rift / Link / Air Link / Steam Link setups, the game must be launched through SteamVR.

---

## Windows installation

### 1. Install Subnautica

Install Subnautica normally through Steam or Epic Games.

In Steam, you can open the game folder through:

```text
Steam
→ Library
→ Subnautica
→ Properties
→ Installed Files
→ Browse
```

A typical Steam installation path is:

```text
C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Subnautica
```

### 2. Install BepInEx for Subnautica

Download BepInEx for Subnautica from:

https://github.com/toebeann/BepInEx.Subnautica/releases

Follow the project's installation instructions and copy it into the Subnautica directory.

After installation, the game directory should contain a `BepInEx` folder and the BepInEx bootstrap files.

After installing BepInEx, it is recommended to launch the game once so that all required folders and configuration files are created.

### 3. Download this mod

Open the **Releases** section of this repository and download the latest **release archive** for SubmersedVR 1.x.

Do not download GitHub's automatically generated archives:

```text
Source code (zip)
Source code (tar.gz)
```

Those contain the project's source code, not the packaged mod.

### 4. Extract the release into the Subnautica folder

Extract the release archive directly into the game's root directory:

```text
...\steamapps\common\Subnautica\
```

If your archive tool asks whether directories should be merged, allow it.

After installation, the plugin should be located approximately here:

```text
Subnautica/
└── BepInEx/
    └── plugins/
        └── SubmersedVR/
            ├── SubmersedVR.dll
            └── ...
```

The release may also contain SteamVR bindings and configuration files that need to be placed outside the plugin directory. This is why the archive should be extracted into the **Subnautica root directory**, rather than manually copying only `SubmersedVR.dll`.

### 5. Do not install conflicting VR mods

Do not install the following mods alongside this plugin:

- the original **SubmersedVR** by Okabintaro as a second copy;
- **Subnautica VR Enhancements Mod**;
- **SN1MC / Motion Controls Mod**.

They modify overlapping VR systems and may conflict with this project.

### 6. Force OpenVR if necessary

For SteamVR headsets, start SteamVR before launching Subnautica.

For Quest/Rift setups using Link or similar connections, add the following launch option to Subnautica if necessary:

```text
-vrmode openvr
```

Then launch the game through Steam/SteamVR.

For **Quest 3 + Steam Link**, connect to SteamVR first, then launch Subnautica from the SteamVR environment.

# Adding music

The simplest option is to use this directory:

```text
Subnautica/BepInEx/plugins/SubmersedVR/Music/
```

Place your local music files there.

Example:

```text
Music/
├── Ambient/
│   ├── 01 - Track.mp3
│   ├── 02 - Track.flac
│   └── Cover.jpg
├── Drum & Bass/
│   ├── Track 01.flac
│   └── Track 02.flac
└── Exploration/
    └── Deep Ocean.mp3
```

The mod automatically scans the library when it starts.

If your music is stored elsewhere, specify the path in:

```text
BepInEx/config/SubmersedVR/music-library-path.txt
```

Use an absolute path to the music directory.

## Requirements

- .NET SDK
- a local Subnautica installation
- BepInEx for Subnautica
- SteamVR for testing

## Build

Clone the repository and specify the path to the local Subnautica installation expected by the project.

The original SubmersedVR uses the `SubnauticaDir` property in:

```text
SubmersedVR/SubmersedVR.csproj
```

Then run:

```bash
dotnet restore
dotnet build
```

Depending on the current project configuration, the build may automatically copy the plugin into the configured Subnautica installation.
